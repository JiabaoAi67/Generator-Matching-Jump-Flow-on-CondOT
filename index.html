One-minute background

Flow matching moves every pixel smoothly along a straight line toward the data (gradual denoising). Generator Matching showed the same marginal probability path can instead be realized by a jump process: pixels don't slide — they teleport at an analytic rate. We reproduced this end-to-end, fixed three pitfalls the paper doesn't document, and found that the jump benefit needs no training: plug the flow's own denoising estimate into the closed-form jump kernel and you get a zero-cost corrector. The remaining sections report when that corrector helps and when it does not.

③ Substitute or complement? Depends on where the grid stacks its steps

The jump corrector's firepower is concentrated at the data end — the (1−t)⁻³ stiffness in its kernel. An EDM grid stacks its steps at the data end too, so grid and corrector address the same error and the corrector's margin shrinks to ≈1%. A uniform grid leaves the data end unresolved and the margin grows to 14%. ImageNet-32 marks the limit of the pattern: only the coarsest grid improves; finer grids reverse sign.

CIFAR-10, formal fixed-sampler re-run (seed 0, TorchMetrics FID). Paired gains: −0.020 (Euler EDM200), −0.026 (Heun2 EDM100, matched NFE), −0.428 (Euler uniform200). Uniform+jump (2.58) is still worse than plain flow on EDM (2.32).
ImageNet-32, formal re-run (CleanFID; values not comparable to CIFAR). Coarse uniform-25 improves by −0.044; uniform-200 (+0.871) and EDM-200 (+1.267) get worse.
Formal re-run: 12 locked runs, fixed sampler, single sampling seed. Reading: the corrector compensates bad time discretization; it shows no consistent cross-model advantage. Grid/solver choice remains the larger effect — switching uniform→EDM alone improves CIFAR flow by 0.69, ~34× the EDM-grid jump effect.

⑤ Speech (F5-TTS): exploratory signal and its replication test

Transplanting the corrector to F5-TTS (mel spectrograms) reveals a mirror image: speech concentrates its difficulty at the noise end (choosing content), so sway sampling (which stacks steps toward the noise end) and the jump corrector act on opposite ends. Sway's usable floor is 5 steps, with a cliff between 5 and 4:

WER % (log scale) vs. sampling steps (NFE). Exploratory phase: at the cliff (NFE 5), adding the jump improved WER in the same direction across three seeds (2.65→2.50).
A preregistered replication on a leakage-free dev protocol (train-split-only statistics, locked seed pairs, corrected transcript casing) did not confirm this: per-pair ΔWER −0.013 / −0.313 / +0.189 pp; pooled −0.046 pp against a preregistered −0.1 pp threshold. The exploratory improvement did not replicate.

⑦ The map: two axes, one open patch

Axis A (done): keep the marginal path, swap the sampler — everything above. A narrow-but-real, training-free first-aid kit.
Axis B (open — verified twice over in the literature): change the marginal path itself. On continuous data, let each coordinate be either still noise or already teleported to its exact clean value — a mixture path with a growing Dirac atom — with three dials: flow↔commitment ratio, commitment granularity (whole frame ↔ per pixel), and source (mask ↔ noise). This one family contains autoregression (hard, ordered commitment), masked generation (SoundStorm/MaskGIT — its discrete cousin), and flow matching (never commit) as corners. The continuous, token-free version has never been built. The demo below is that path family.
